Chapter 4 Writing Project

Overview

  • Task : You will research your family history through interviews and available records, then write a three-section personal history in narrative form. Your writing will describe your immediate family, explore your ancestral heritage with specific stories and connections to your current life, and offer wisdom for future generations.
  • Purpose: To reflect on your family history, connect it to your identity, and communicate those insights through organized, narrative writing.
  • Length : 750–1000 words

Instructions

Step 1: Research

Contact family members to gather names, dates, stories, and background information. Apply family interview techniques from this week's chapter. Use systematic organization for genealogical data (names, dates, relationships, stories). Balance primary sources (family interviews, documents) with secondary sources when available. Remember that incomplete information is normal in family research. Obtain permission before including personal information about living relatives. Avoid full birth dates if privacy is a concern.

Step 2: Organize

Sort your findings by the three required sections. Plan your word distribution: My Family Background (200–300 words), My Ancestral Heritage (400–500 words), My Legacy and Advice (150–200 words). Transform biographical facts into narrative elements that tell your family's story rather than simply listing information. Focus most heavily on developing the ancestral heritage section.

Step 3: Draft

Write your three sections in narrative format

  • My Family Background: Write 1–2 paragraphs describing your immediate family, focusing on family story rather than biographical details. Include cultural context and defining characteristics.
  • My Ancestral Heritage: Write 2–3 well-developed paragraphs including where ancestors lived, at least two specific ancestral stories, concrete examples of how ancestral experiences influence your family today, and a brief description of your research process.
  • My Legacy and Advice: Share three specific pieces of advice for future generations with explanations of why each matters.

Step 4: Revise

Transform factual information into engaging storytelling using complete sentences and paragraphs. Use specific details and examples to bring ancestors to life. Make connections between ancestral experiences and present-day life. Address missing information honestly rather than avoiding gaps in knowledge. Review your draft to confirm you have created engaging narrative that connects past experiences to your current life and values.

A note on privacy before you submit: the family information in this project, names, locations, relationships, and personal stories, belongs to your family. Do not enter identifying information about your relatives into an AI tool. If you use AI for grammar checking, replace names with placeholders such as "my grandmother" before pasting your text. Restore the names yourself before submitting. This protects your family members and reflects the principle that you are responsible for every decision about how their information is shared.

Step 5: Requirements Checklist

Before you submit, verify:

  • Three distinct sections with clear headings: "My Family Background," "My Ancestral Heritage," and "My Legacy and Advice"
  • Written in complete paragraphs using narrative format (not lists of names and dates)
  • Includes at least two specific ancestral stories with connections to your present life
  • Contains three pieces of advice for future generations with explanations
  • Saved as PDF with filename: w04_personal_history-FirstName_LastName

Step 6: Submit the Assignment

  • Submit your assignment to W04 Writing Project in Canvas, which is due by the end of the week.

English Writing: Mastering the Basics

Commas in a List of Items

When you want to list several nouns in a sentence, you separate each word with a comma. This allows the reader to pause after each item and identify which words are included in the grouping. When you list items in a sentence, put a comma after each noun, then add the word and before the last item. You do not need to include a comma after the last item.

We will need to get flour, tomatoes, and cheese at the store.
The pizza will be topped with olives, peppers, and pineapple chunks.

Rule of Three

Effective writers often implement the “rule of three,” since groups of three elements are more memorable and more satisfying to readers than any other number. Try to use a series of three when providing examples, grouping adjectives, or generating a list.

AI Usage Policy

This assignment develops family research skills and authentic personal reflection about your ancestral heritage. AI cannot know your family history, conduct interviews with your relatives, or provide genuine insights about how your ancestors have shaped your life. The learning objectives focus on applying research techniques to gather family stories and creating meaningful connections between past and present that only come from your personal knowledge and reflection.

PROHIBITED : Having AI write content, ideas, or analysis for you

  • Why prohibited: AI cannot conduct interviews, generate authentic family stories, or provide genuine insights about your family connections and heritage

ASK FIRST : Translation assistance or major editing questions

  • Why restricted: Translation support should preserve your family's original meaning and cultural context, and major edits should maintain your voice

APPROVED : Grammar and spelling identification after completing your draft

  • Why allowed: Technical writing support does not compromise your authentic family content and helps all students communicate their stories clearly
Approved AI Prompts

Use these prompts to stay in control of your work:

  • Grammar Check : "Please identify grammar and spelling errors in this paragraph without changing my ideas: [paste your text]"
  • Clarity Review : "Which sentences in this paragraph could be clearer? Please point them out without rewriting: [paste your text]"
  • Translation Support : "Help me express this family story more clearly in English while keeping the original meaning and cultural context: [paste specific sentence]"
