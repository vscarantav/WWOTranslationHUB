Chapter 2 Activity: AI Prompt Comparison

Overview

  • Task: You will write two different AI prompts on the same topic, review the responses you receive, and compare how your prompt choices influenced the results.
  • Purpose: To practice writing effective prompts and reflect on how input choices shape AI-generated responses
  • Length : Two AI responses (saved as submitted) + one short written reflection (150–200 words)
  • Graded on: Completion. To receive full credit, submit both prompts and your reflection by the deadline.

Background: Prompts Work Like Previewing

In this week's reading, you learned that how you approach a text before you read it changes what you find inside it. Previewing means looking at headings, introductions, and key features before reading closely. It sets you up to understand more. The same idea applies to AI. The words you use in a prompt set conditions for what AI produces.

A vague prompt returns a generic response. A more specific prompt returns something more useful, though still not perfect.

This activity gives you a chance to see that difference for yourself. One thing to keep in mind as you work through this activity: a well-structured prompt is not automatically a responsible prompt. You can write a prompt that includes a clear role, specific details, useful context, and a defined format, and that prompt can still be a dependence trap if it asks AI to do thinking you were meant to do yourself. The distinction between a growth accelerator and a dependence trap is not about prompt quality. It is about whether the prompt keeps the core work with you or gives it away. As you compare your two AI responses, notice not just which response is more useful, but whether either prompt asks AI to replace thinking that would have built a skill you need.

Instructions

Step 1: Choose a Topic

Pick one of the following, or choose your own topic related to your current coursework or work life:

  • How to study more effectively
  • How to write a professional email
  • How to manage time when you have many responsibilities
  • A concept from another course you are taking right now

Step 2: Write a Weak Prompt

Write a prompt that is vague and short. Give AI as little information as possible. For example: "Tell me about time management."

Submit your prompt to any AI tool you have access to: ChatGPT, Claude, Google Gemini, or Microsoft Copilot.

Step 3: Write a Stronger Prompt

Now write a second prompt on the same topic. This time, add at least two of these four elements:

  • Role: Tell AI who it should act as. For example: "Act as a study skills coach."
  • Details: Give AI specific information about your situation. For example: "I work full time and study in the evenings."
  • Context: Tell AI what you are trying to accomplish. For example: "I am preparing for exams in two courses."
  • Format: Tell AI how to organize its response. For example: "Give me three practical tips in plain language."

Submit this second prompt to the same AI tool.

Step 4: Write Your Reflection

In 150–200 words, answer these three questions in your own words:

  1. What was the most noticeable difference between the two AI responses?
  2. Which element you added (role, details, context, or format) seemed to make the biggest difference, and why do you think that is?
  3. Ada Okonkwo, from this week's case study, used AI to explain unfamiliar American idioms after she had already read the text herself. How does the idea of reading first and using AI second connect to what you observed in this activity?

Step 5: Requirements Checklist

Before you submit, confirm you have included:

  • Your weak prompt
  • Your stronger prompt
  • A 150–200 word reflection answering all three questions

Step 6: Submit the Assignment

  • Submit your assignment to W02: AI Prompt Comparison Activity in Canvas, which is due by the end of the week.

A Note on Honesty

This activity asks you to use AI on purpose and observe what happens. That is the assignment. You do not need to hide your AI use or worry that using it means you did something wrong. What matters is that the reflection, the part where you notice, compare, and think, is yours. AI cannot do that part for you, because AI did not watch itself respond to your prompts. You did.

Tips for Being a Successful Online Student

Seek Help When Needed: Your instructor cares about you and has the resources to help you succeed. Please email if you need support with content, technology issues, or personal challenges. The sooner you reach out to instructors, the better they can help you. Waiting weeks to communicate your struggles gives you few options for help.

Tips for Improving Your Writing

Revise and Edit: Good writing often involves multiple drafts and revisions. Writing a draft and waiting a day to evaluate your writing will help you see mistakes you could not see immediately after writing. Slowly reading your work aloud will help you discover edits you need to make.
