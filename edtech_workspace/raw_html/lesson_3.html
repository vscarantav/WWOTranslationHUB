Chapter 1 Draft: Your Life Story

Overview

  • Task : You will select one significant person or life experience and write a focused reflection that demonstrates thesis development and paragraph organization skills. You will create a clear thesis statement about what this influence taught you, support it with specific examples from your experience, and connect this lesson to two future goals.
  • Purpose: To practice reflection and connect a meaningful lesson to your future goals through clear, organized writing.
  • Length : 450–650 words

Instructions

Step 1: Select

Choose one person or experience you feel comfortable sharing. Students from different cultural backgrounds may approach personal sharing differently — professional or community experiences work well if family topics feel too personal. Create a simple thesis: “Through [specific way], [Person/Event] taught me [lesson].”

Examples:

  • “My uncle’s fishing wisdom taught me patience. This happened through his daily practice of waiting for the right moment to cast nets.”
  • “Working in my family’s market taught me customer service. I learned this through daily interactions with neighbors from our community.”
  • “My supervisor at the clinic taught me responsibility. This happened through her example of checking every detail before helping patients.”

Step 2: Draft

Write your complete reflection independently. Include background, specific examples, personal impact, and current application. Add two future goals connected to your lesson. Remember Ada’s experience with directness in academic writing and Mateo’s thesis development process from your reading.

Sentence Starters:

  • Opening sentences: “When I first met [person]...” or “During [time period]...”
  • Example transitions: “This experience taught me...” or “Through this influence, I learned...”
  • Impact statements: “Today, this lesson affects how I...” or “This influence continues to guide my decisions when...”

Step 3: Review

Check your thesis support and paragraph organization. Before you open any AI tool, ask yourself one question: Have I completed the core thinking myself? Your draft should already include your thesis, your chosen person or experience, your specific examples, and your two future goals. AI enters at the end of this process. It does not enter at the beginning.

The approved prompts below are designed as growth accelerators. They ask AI to identify issues. You then decide how to fix those issues. Avoid the corresponding dependence trap. The dependence trap is asking AI to rewrite a sentence or improve your phrasing for you. A prompt such as “Make this sentence clearer” is a rewrite request. A prompt such as “Which sentence in this paragraph might be hard for a reader to follow?” is a feedback request. The type of prompt you use determines whether your voice remains yours.

Use AI for grammar and spelling help only if needed, following these approved prompts:

  • “Please identify any grammar or spelling errors in this paragraph. Point out the errors but do not rewrite my sentences: [paste paragraph]”
  • “Which sentences in this paragraph might be confusing to readers? Identify unclear sentences without rewriting them: [paste paragraph]”
  • “I am trying to express this idea in English: [paste sentence]. What are 2–3 different ways I could say this more clearly while keeping my original meaning?”

Step 4: Submit

Save with correct filename, add AI attribution statement if used (see the Required Attribution section at the end of this assignment), and submit to Canvas.

Step 5: Requirements Checklist

Before you submit, verify:

  • Clear thesis statement identifies one person or event and one lesson learned
  • 3–4 paragraphs with specific examples supporting your thesis
  • Two future goals that connect to your reflection
  • Personal experiences and insights written entirely by you
  • File named “W01-FirstName-LastName” and submitted to Canvas

Step 6: Submit the Assignment

  • Submit your assignment to W01 Draft: Your Life Story in Canvas, which is due by the end of the week.

Success Criteria

Excellent work will:

  • Present a focused thesis that clearly states one person or event and one specific lesson
  • Support the thesis with concrete, specific examples from personal experience
  • Connect the reflection meaningfully to realistic future goals

AI Usage Policy

This assignment is a process task . The learning objective is not the document you submit. The objective is the act of examining your own life, identifying a meaningful pattern, and expressing that pattern as a thesis. AI cannot retrieve your memories. AI cannot know the people who shaped you. AI cannot understand the significance of the moments you are describing.

If AI writes your content, ideas, or analysis, you have not completed the assignment. This is true regardless of how polished the result looks. Write your full draft before you use any AI tool. After your draft is complete, you may use AI for grammar and spelling identification only.

If you use Grammarly or a similar tool, set it to identify errors only. Do not allow it to rewrite, rephrase, or suggest replacement sentences for any part of your draft. Accepting AI rewrites, even partial ones, replaces your voice with generated text. It also removes the thesis and paragraph practice this assignment is designed to build.

PROHIBITED : Having AI write content, ideas, or analysis for you

  • Why prohibited: AI cannot provide authentic insights about your personal experiences, relationships, or how life events have influenced your character and goals

ASK FIRST : Translation assistance

  • Why restricted: Translation support should preserve your original experiences and personal voice while meeting academic writing standards. Ask your instructor before using AI for translation so you can confirm your original meaning is intact.

APPROVED : Grammar and spelling identification after completing your draft

  • Why allowed: Technical writing support does not compromise your authentic reflection and helps all students communicate their personal insights clearly
Approved AI Prompts

Use these prompts to stay in control of your work:

    Grammar Check: “Please identify grammar and spelling errors in this paragraph without changing my ideas: [paste your text]”
  • Clarity Review: “Which sentences in this paragraph could be clearer? Please point them out without rewriting: [paste your text]”
  • Translation Support: “Help me express this idea more clearly in English while keeping my original meaning: [paste specific sentence]”
AI Use Declaration & Attribution

At the top of your submission, include one of the following statements:

  • “I did not use AI assistance on this assignment.”
  • “[AI tool name] helped with [specific function] after I completed my draft. All ideas and content are my own work.”

Please note that you may be asked to describe how you used AI and to share transcripts from your AI conversations. These conversations with instructors are meant to help you understand the appropriate way to use AI to improve your learning.

Why This Assignment Matters

This reflection develops thesis statement and paragraph skills you will use in all college writing. It also helps you analyze your personal growth and set meaningful goals. These skills transfer directly to job interviews, performance reviews, and professional development conversations.
