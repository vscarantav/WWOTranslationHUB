Chapter 2 Writing Project

Overview

  • Task: You will select one specific instance when someone made a positive impact on your life, then write a formal thank-you letter to that person. Your letter must use specific, concrete details rather than general statements and follow professional correspondence format. This assignment applies Week 2's reading strategies to your own writing process.
  • Purpose: To practice writing with specific details and apply effective communication strategies in a professional format.
  • Length: 250 words

Instructions

Step 1: Select

Choose one person and one specific moment when they helped you. Focus on one moment that you can describe in detail rather than trying to thank someone for everything they have done. Write down the exact details: what happened, when, where, and how it affected you.

Step 2: Plan your Writing

What exactly did they do? How did I feel before and after? What was the concrete impact? Consider what specific story you want to tell. Use concrete details like "spent three hours helping me practice my presentation" rather than general phrases like "helped me so much."

Step 3: Draft

Write your letter using complete letter format: date, appropriate salutation, 2–3 body paragraphs telling your specific story, professional closing, and signature. Apply the same active engagement you learned for reading. Focus on specific details and your authentic voice. Complete this entirely on your own.

Step 5: Requirements Checklist

Before you submit, verify:

  • Letter focuses on ONE specific moment or experience (not general appreciation)
  • Includes all format elements: date, salutation, body (2–3 paragraphs), closing, signature Uses specific details instead of general language ("tutored me for two hours" not "helped me")
  • Sounds like your authentic voice (no template language or AI-generated content)
  • Saved as "w02_letter_FirstnameLastname.docx" and submitted to Canvas

Step 6: Submit the Assignment

  • Submit your assignment to W02 Writing Project in Canvas, which is due by the end of the week.

Success Criteria

Excellent work will:

  • Tell the story of one specific moment with concrete details a reader can visualize
  • Use professional letter format appropriate for your relationship with the recipient
  • Demonstrate your authentic writing voice while avoiding general or template language

AI Usage Policy

This assignment develops your ability to express authentic gratitude through specific personal narratives and professional correspondence skills. AI cannot know your real relationships, personal experiences, or genuine feelings about how someone helped you. The learning objectives focus on building authentic voice, specific narrative techniques, and meaningful reflection that only come from drawing on your own memories and emotions. If you use Grammarly or a similar tool, set it to identify errors only. Do not allow it to rewrite, rephrase, or suggest replacement sentences for any part of your draft. Accepting AI rewrites, even partial ones, replaces your voice with generated text.

PROHIBITED : Having AI write content, ideas, or personal experiences for you

  • Why prohibited : AI cannot provide genuine memories, authentic emotional responses, or real details about your relationships and experiences

ASK FIRST : Translation assistance or major editing questions

  • Why restricted : Translation support should preserve your authentic voice and personal meaning while supporting multilingual students

APPROVED : Grammar and spelling identification after completing your draft

  • Why allowed : Technical writing support does not compromise your authentic personal story and helps all students communicate their experiences clearly

English Writing: Mastering the Basics

Avoiding Overly General Words

Specific words and images make your writing more interesting to read. Whenever possible, avoid overly general words in your writing. Instead, try to replace general language with nouns, verbs, and modifiers that convey details. Use words that bring your words to life. Add words that provide color, texture, sound, and even smell to your writing.

General words: A big dog walked down the loud street.
Specific words: The golden retriever scurried through the crisp fall leaves.

Avoiding Slang

Slang describes informal words that are considered nonstandard English. Slang often changes with fads and may be familiar to only a specific group of people. Most people use slang when they speak or in personal correspondences, such as e-mails, text messages, and instant messages. Slang is appropriate between friends in an informal context but should be avoided in formal academic writing.
