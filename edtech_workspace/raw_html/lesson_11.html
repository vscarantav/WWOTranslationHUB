Introduction

The Quantitative Reasoning Process is useful for comparing options. Among the different quantitative tools used to make this type of comparison is a system of equations .

A system of equations is a list of two or more equations that use the same input and output variables.

Both graphs or algebra can be used to solve for the values of the variables that will satisfy both equations.

1. Understand the Problem

Here is an example: Sophia and Emmy are in the jungle and need to find a way back to their hotel. They must decide whether to rent a monster truck or a motor boat. To make this decision they will need to compare the costs of using these different methods of transportation.

2. Identify Variables & Assumptions

This problem contains the following variables:

  • The number of kilometers Sophia and Emmy need to travel

  • The total cost of transportation out of the jungle

You can use the following assumptions:

  • Sophia and Emmy can drive either the truck or the boat

  • Sophia can use her emergency credit card that was, thankfully, not swallowed by a black caiman

You can set up two related equations since the variables are the same in both cases: the total cost of transportation out of the jungle and the number of kilometers. The focus of this problem is on money and distance, regardless of which option they choose.

3. Apply Quantitative Tools

Each scenario can be modeled with a mathematical equation.

The total cost, \( y \), of the truck, is \( $37.50+$0.88\times\text{kilometers: }y=37.50+0.88x \)


The total cost, \( y \), of the boat, is \( $60.00+$0.19\times\text{kilometers: }y=60+0.19x \)

The input value, \( x \), in each of the equations, is the number of kilometers they travel. The output value, \( y \), is the total cost of transportation.

The two equations in this example are linear equations (see Lesson 7). The rate of change (or slope) in each equation is constant. When plotted, this type of equation results in a straight line.

Look more carefully at the graph of the two lines representing the situation. The blue line represents the cost of the monster truck as the kilometers increase. The red line represents the cost of the boat as the kilometers increase.

The two lines cross at around 33 kilometers on the \( x \)-axis and just above $65 on the \( y \)-axis.

The truck is least expensive if Sophia and Emmy travel less than 33 kilometers. If they travel more than 33 kilometers, then the boat is the least expensive.

4. Make an Informed Decision

Sophia and Emmy need more information to make an informed decision. They need to know the distance from their jungle position back to town.

After asking for more information, they find that the dirt road back to the hotel is about 40 kilometers long and the river trip is 38 kilometers. Use the equations to compute the cost of each rental vehicle.

5. Evaluate Your Reasoning

Sophia and Emmy tie the boat up at the town marina and ask for directions to their hotel. After finding out that they still have 5 kilometers to walk, they rethink their decision.

Sophia and Emmy realize they made an incorrect assumption in their application of the Quantitative Reasoning Process. They mistakenly assumed that both modes of transportation would get them back to their hotel. Since they didn’t realize that travel by boat would also require a 5-kilometer walk, they made a decision that didn’t turn out to be the best.

Because the difference in price between the rentals was relatively small, they now wish they had rented the monster truck and driven straight to the hotel.

The remainder of this lesson will focus on the powerful quantitative tool, systems of equations, that we used to understand Sophia and Emmy’s situation.

Several different techniques can be used to solve systems of equations. We will look at two of them: solving systems of equations with graphs and solving systems of equations with algebra.

Solving Systems with Graphs

The graph of an equation represents all the points that satisfy the equation. When we are solving a system of equations, we want to find a point that satisfies all of the equations in the system. This means that if you graph both equations in a system of two equations on a graph, you can find the solution to the system by finding all points where the graphs intersect. An intersection point satisfies both equations, so it represents the solution we are looking for.

Example 1

Graph the following two equations to solve the system of equations:

$$ 3x + 2y = 5 $$
$$ x - y = 0 $$
Solution

Find a point \( (x, y) \) that satisfies both equations. The point you are looking for will be the point where the two graphs intersect. If you solve each equation for \( y \) , you will see that they fit the pattern of a linear function, and you will be able to graph the lines.

Equation 1:

$$ 3x + 2y = 5 $$
$$ 2y = -3x + 5 $$
$$ y = \frac{-3}{2}x + \frac{5}{2} $$

Equation 2:

$$ x-y=0 $$

$$ -y=-x $$

$$ y=x $$

Equation 1 can be represented with a line with a \( y \)-intercept of 5/2 and a slope of −3/2. Equation 2 gives the graph of a line with a \( y \)-intercept of 0 and a slope of 1. If you plot both lines on the same graph, they look like this:

The solution to the system of equations is represented on the graph by the point where the two lines intersect. By looking at the graph of these two lines, you can see that they cross at the point \( (1,1) \). This means \( x=1 \), \( y=1 \) is the solution to the system of equations.

Check your answer.

Verify that \( x = 1 \), \( y = 1 \) is the solution to the system by checking that \( (1,1) \) is exactly the point where they cross. To do this, substitute these values for \( x \) and \( y \) in both equations and make sure that both mathematical statements are true.

Equation 1:

$$ 3x + 2y = 5 $$
$$ 3(1) + 2(1) = 5 $$
$$ 3 + 2 = 5 $$
$$ 5 = 5 $$
$$ \text{True Statement} $$

Equation 2:

$$ x - y = 0 $$
$$ 1 - 1 = 0 $$
$$ 0 = 0 $$
$$ \text{True Statement} $$

Point \( (1,1) \) is the only point where the two lines cross and the values \( x = 1 \) and \( y = 1 \) are the only values for \( x \) and \( y \) that will yield true statements for both equations. When there is only one single unique solution, the system of equations is independent .



Example 2

Graph the following two equations to solve the system of equations:

$$ -x + 2y = 4 $$
$$ -3x + 6y = -18 $$
Solution

Solve each equation for \( y \). Each one fits the pattern of a linear function and can be graphed with lines.

Equation 1:

$$ -x + 2y = 4 $$
$$ 2y = x + 4 $$
$$ y = \frac{x + 4}{2} $$
$$ y = \frac{1}{2}x + \frac{4}{2} $$
$$ y = \frac{1}{2}x + 2 $$

The first equation can be graphed as a line with a slope of 1/2 and a \( y \) -intercept of 2.

Equation 2:

$$ -3x + 6y = -18 $$
$$ 6y = 3x - 18 $$
$$ y = \frac{3x - 18}{6} $$
$$ y = \frac{3}{6}x - \frac{18}{6} $$
$$ y = \frac{1}{2}x - 3 $$

The second equation can be graphed as a line with a slope of 1/2 and a \( y \)-intercept of -3. Plotted on the same graph, they look like this:

The graph makes it look as if the lines are parallel, meaning they will never cross or intersect. The equations demonstrate that the lines are parallel because the two lines have the same slope. This means that there is no \( x,y \) pair that will satisfy both equations and thus there is no solution to this system of linear equations. When there is no solution to a system of equations, the system of equations is inconsistent .


Example: Systems of Equations

Types of Solutions

There are three types of solutions for systems of equations:

Independent Systems:
An independent system is formed when two lines intersect at one point. The point where the lines intersect is the solution to the system.


Inconsistent Systems:
An inconsistent system is formed when two lines are parallel and never intersect. Inconsistent systems have no solution.


Dependent Systems:
A dependent system is formed when the two equations in the system actually represent the same line. Every point on the line is a solution to the system. The solution has an infinite number of solutions.



Solving Systems with Algebra

Graphing systems of equations helps visualize the system and the solutions but unless the equations and solutions are fairly simple accurate results are difficult to obtain. Systems of equations are also solved using the techniques of algebra. One way to solve a system of equations is known as the Substitution Method . To solve a system of equations using the substitution method, eliminate one variable from one of the equations by substituting an expression for that variable from the other equation.

Example 3

Use the substitution method to solve the system of equations:

$$ 2x - y = -5 $$
$$ 3x + 5y = 12 $$
Solution

To solve this system using the substitution method, solve the first equation for \( y \):

$$ 2x - y = -5 $$
$$ -y = -2x - 5 $$
$$ y = 2x + 5 $$

Now substitute the expression \( 2x + 5 \) in for the \( y \) in the other equation:

$$ 3x + 5y = 12 $$
$$ 3x + 5(2x + 5) = 12 $$

Because the equation only has \( x \)'s in it, solve for \( x \):

$$ 3x + 5(2x + 5) = 12 $$
$$ 3x + 10x + 25 = 12 $$
$$ 13x + 25 = 12 $$
$$ 13x = 12 - 25 $$
$$ 13x = -13 $$
$$ x = \frac{-13}{13} $$
$$ x = -1 $$

\( x = -1 \)

Substitute this value for the x in the y-expression found earlier, \( y = 2x + 5 \):

$$ y = 2x + 5 $$
$$ y = 2(-1) + 5 $$
$$ y = -2 + 5 $$
$$ y = 3 $$

The solution to this system of equations is \( x = −1 \), \( y = 3 \). This system of equations is independent because there is only one single solution. The graph verifies this solution because the lines intersect at the point \( (−1, 3) \).


Example 4

Use the substitution method to solve the system of equations:

$$ 6x - 9y = -13 $$
$$ 18x - 3y = 1 $$
Solution

Solve for the \( y \) in the second equation:

$$ 18x - 3y = 1 $$
$$ -3y = -18x + 1 $$
$$ y = \frac{-18x + 1}{-3} $$
$$ y = \frac{-18}{-3}x + \frac{1}{-3} $$
$$ y = 6x - \frac{1}{3} $$

Now that you know \( y \) in terms of \( x \), substitute this into the first equation:

$$ 6x - 9y = -13 $$
$$ 6x - 9(6x - \frac{1}{3}) = -13 $$
$$ 6x - 54x + \frac{9}{3} = -13 $$
$$ -48x + 3 = -13 $$
$$ -48x = -13 - 3 $$
$$ -48x = -16 $$
$$ x = \frac{-16}{-48} $$
$$ x = \frac{1}{3} $$

Now substitute 1/3 in for \( x \) in any of the equations to find \( y \):

$$ y = 6x - \frac{1}{3} $$
$$ y = 6 \left( \frac{1}{3} \right) - \frac{1}{3} $$
$$ y = \frac{6}{3} - \frac{1}{3} $$
$$ y = \frac{6-1}{3} $$
$$ y = \frac{5}{3} $$

This system of equations is independent. The only solution that works for both equations is \( x=\frac{1}{3} \) , \( y=\frac{5}{3} \). The two lines cross at the point \( (\frac{1}{3},\frac{5}{3}) \).

Solving in Context

Piano Sales Example

1. Understand the Problem

Jasper loves music and has recently been hired as a piano salesman. His new boss asked him to choose a commission plan. He can choose Plan A or Plan B. For Plan A, Jasper gets a base salary of $25,000 a year plus a $500 commission for each piano he sells. In Plan B, Jasper gets a base salary of $30,000 a year plus a $200 commission for each piano .

2. Identify Variables & Assumptions

Jasper identifies two variables:

  • The number of pianos sold per year

  • The total amount of money he earns in the year

3. Apply Quantitative Tools

Because Plan A and Plan B have the same two variables, Jasper can model his situation using a system of equations to better understand the decision he needs to make.

  • Plan A: The money he earns in one year = $25,000 + $500 for each piano

  • Plan B: The money he earns in one year = $30,000 + $200 for each piano

This can be turned into the following system of equations where the input variable, \( x \), represents the number of pianos Jasper sells in a year, and the output variable, \( y \), represents the total amount of money he earns in one year.

$$ y = 500x + 25000 $$
$$ y = 200x + 30000 $$

Because both equations are already solved for\( y \) , substitute \( 200x + 30000 \) for \( y \) in the first equation and solve for \( x \):

$$ 200x + 30000 = 500x + 25000 $$
$$ 200x - 500x = 25000 - 30000 $$
$$ -300x = -5000 $$
$$ x = \frac{-5000}{-300} $$
$$ x = 16.67 $$

Now substitute \( x = 16.67 \) into any of the equations to find \( y \):

$$ y = 500x + 25000 $$
$$ y = 500(16.67) + 25000 $$
$$ y = 33335 $$

The solution to the system is \( x = 16.67 \),\( y = $33,335 \).

The following graph of this system supports this solution:

4. Make an Informed Decision

From this solution, Jasper knows if he sells around 16 or 17 pianos the two plans pay about the same and he would make around $33,335 per year. If he sells more than 16 pianos, Plan A is a better plan because he earns more money in a year. If he sells 16 pianos or fewer, Plan B is a better plan.

He believes he can sell at least 2 pianos per month. Since this is a total of 24 pianos he decides to select Plan A.

5. Evaluate Your Reasoning

Before telling his boss his decision, Jasper decides to ask some of the other salesmen how many pianos they sold the previous year. He talks to three of them and finds that they sold 18, 31, and 22 pianos each. Because these values are all more than 17, Jasper is satisfied with his decision to choose Plan A and communicates his decision to his boss.

Systems and Trendlines

Often, the equations that make up a system of equations are obtained from data using a trendline. The following example looks at an example from business: the break-even point.

1. Understand the Problem

Jeff and Lucas opened a pizzeria. They have been open for 10 months now and are doing some calculations to determine what they can do to improve their profits. Currently, they charge $10 for the large pizza, their bestselling item. During the 10 months their restaurant has been open, they collected the following data:


A B C D
1 Month Pizzas Sold Total Costs Total Revenue
2 February 830 $10,240.21 $9,208.84
3 March 851 $11,013.52 $9,551.70
4 April 902 $10,985.39 $9,831.73
5 May 1120 $12,791.87 $12,377.68
6 June 1291 $13,682.76 $14,239.06
7 July 917 $11,441.86 $10,556.98
8 August 1060 $12,004.55 $11,629.34
9 September 1402 $13,716.38 $15,681.21
10 October 1289 $12,940.14 $14,461.54
11 November 1305 $13,850.56 $14,100.33

They want to answer two questions:

  • How many pizzas do we need to sell to break even each month?

  • Should we change the price of our large pizza?

2. Identify Variables & Assumptions

Jeff and Lucas identify the following variables:

  • The number of pizzas sold each month

  • Total cost each month (how much money they have to pay to keep the business open)

  • Total revenue each month (the total amount of money they bring in during one month)

  • Total profit each month (total revenue minus total cost)

They make the following assumptions:

  • They will break even if their monthly revenue is equal to their monthly costs.

  • If they change the price of the pizza, the number of pizzas sold will stay the same.

3. Apply Quantitative Tools

Jeff and Lucas create the following scatterplot and trendlines to compare their revenue and costs. The input variable, \( x \) , represents the number of pizzas sold in one month. The output variable, \( y \), represents money. We can see the data and the trendline for both the total revenue (orange) and total cost (blue).

These two trendlines give them a system of equations:

$$ y = 10.893x + 217.7 $$
$$ y = 5.8929x + 5803.9 $$

Jeff and Lucas use substitution to solve the system of equations. Because both equations are already solved for \( y \), they can set the equations equal to each other and solve for \( x \):

$$ 10.893x + 217.7 = 5.8929x + 5803.90 $$
$$ 10.893x - 5.8929x = 5803.90 - 217.7 $$
$$ 5.0001x = 5586.20 $$
$$ x = \frac{5586.20}{5.0001} $$
$$ x = 1117.2177 $$

They can then substitute this value of \( x \) back into any of the equations to solve for \( y \). They use the first equation:

$$ y = 10.893x + 217.7 $$
$$ y = 10.893(1117.2177) + 217.7 $$
$$ y = 12387.5524 $$

The solution to the system of equations is

$$ x = 1117.2177, y = 12387.5524. $$

Jeff and Lucas know that \( x \) represents the number of pizzas sold and \( y \) represents a dollar amount. This solution means that if Jeff and Lucas sell 1,117 pizzas, they will break even, and their revenue and costs will both be about $12,387.55.

4. Make an Informed Decision

These calculations answer Jeff and Lucas’ first question: How many pizzas do they need to sell to break even each month? They need to sell around 1,117 pizzas per month. Because they are closed on Sunday, their pizzeria is open approximately 26 days per month. Jeff and Lucas divide the number of pizzas by the number of days and get the result 42.96. Rounding this number to the nearest whole number means they need to sell an average of 43 pizzas every day to break even.

Looking back at the data, Jeff and Lucas notice that they sold more than 1,117 pizzas in 5 of the past 10 months: May, June, September, October, and November. They are concerned that they sold fewer than 1,117 pizzas in the other 5 months. However, sales for the most recent three months are good. They hope that this upward trend in pizza sales continues as their pizzeria becomes more popular.

To answer the question of whether they should increase the price of the large pizza, they notice that in the past three months, although they have sold more than 1,117 pizzas, their total revenue was not much larger than their total costs. That means their profits were relatively low. They decide to increase the price of the large pizza to $11. They hope the extra revenue will increase their profits.

5. Evaluate Your Reasoning

As Jeff and Lucas evaluate their reasoning, they re-examine their assumptions. One of their assumptions was: “If we change the price of the pizza, the number of pizzas sold will stay the same.” As they think about this assumption, they realize it is probably not very accurate. Reducing prices would probably increase sales while increasing prices would probably decrease sales. Because they decided to increase the price of the large pizza to $11, Jeff and Lucas realize their sales may decrease. They decide to try the higher price for three months and then re-evaluate their decision.

After three more months of sales, Jeff and Lucas reevaluate their decision. In those three months, they sold 1,410, 1,511, and 1,620 pizzas. So far, it appears that the increase in their prices has not decreased their sales significantly. Their sales remain above the 1,117 pizzas they calculated that they needed to sell to break even. Because they raised the price of the large pizza they find that their revenue has increased, so their profits are also increasing. Jeff and Lucas decide to do more advertising. They know that if they can sell more pizzas their profits will continue to increase.

Nonlinear Systems

So far, all of the systems of equations in this lesson have involved linear equations. Linear systems are the most common type found in real-life problems. Lines are simple, in both a graph and an equation. You may often choose to model situations with lines. This is especially useful when the solution does not have to be precise, like rounding up to the nearest pizza or the nearest dollar.

However, sometimes situations need more precision. In this case it is helpful to use non-linear models. There are ways to solve non-linear systems with algebra, however this lesson covers only non-linear systems using graphs.

Here is an example of a nonlinear system of equations:

$$ y = 8x^2 + 12x + 1 $$
$$ y = 2(4)^x + 3 $$

Notice that the first equation is a quadratic function and the second equation is an exponential function. The following result is obtained when these functions are graphed:


Notice there are three solutions to this system of equations because the graphs intersect at three different points. From the graph, it is difficult to determine the exact location of the intersection points. You may estimate that they are somewhere around the points \( (−1.6,3.5) \), \( (0.3,6) \), and \( (2.8,95) \).
