Manipulating Fractions

A knowledge of fractions is useful in many real-world situations. For example, you use fractions to follow recipes in cooking and baking. Most recipes require ingredient measurements that are less than a whole.

Fractions represent a part of a whole. Parts of a whole are expressed using a fraction bar, or line, with numbers on the top and bottom. The number on the top of the fraction is called the numerator, and represents part of the whole. The number on the bottom of the fraction is called the denominator, which is the whole . One of the easiest ways to understand the use of fractions is to think of dividing a round pizza into triangular pieces.

In the following exercises, you will review the rules to follow when adding, subtracting, multiplying, and dividing fractions. These are brief examples and are intended to be a quick review of principles you have learned before.

Adding Fractions

To add fractions, do the following:

  • Find a common denominator (the number on the bottom of the fraction).

  • Add the numerators (the numbers on top) and keep the common denominator of the fraction the same.

Here is an example.

Example 1

Add.

\frac{3}{4} + \frac{2}{3}
Solution

To find a common denominator, you need to figure out what is the smallest number that can be evenly divided by both denominators.

In Example 1, the denominators are 4 and 3.

Step 1: Find a denominator that is divisible by both 4 and 3. The smallest number that can be evenly divided by 4 and 3 is 12, so the common denominator is 12.

Step 2 : Multiply both fractions by 1, written as a number divided by itself, so that you can get the common denominator of 12:

\displaylines{\frac{3}{4} + \frac{2}{3} = \frac{3}{4}\cdot\frac{3}{3} + \frac{2}{3}\cdot\frac{4}{4} \\ = \frac{9}{12} + \frac{8}{12} \\ = \frac {9 + 8}{12} \\ = \frac{17}{12}}

Notice that after you find the common denominator (12), you add the numerators of the fractions, but keep the common denominator of 12 on the bottom of the fraction.

Example 2

Add.

\frac{17}{9} + \frac{8}{3}
Solution

  • First, find a common denominator. In this case, the denominators of the two fractions are 9 and 3. Find a denominator that is divisible by both 9 and 3. The smallest number that can be evenly divided by 9 and 3 is 9. So the common denominator is 9.

  • Multiply the second fraction by 1, written as a number divided by itself, to get the common denominator of 9.

  • Notice that after you find the common denominator of 9, you add the numerators of the fractions, but keep the common denominator of 9 on the bottom of the fraction.

\displaylines{\frac{17}{9}+\frac{8}{3}=\frac{17}{9}+\frac{8}{3}\cdot\frac{3}{3} \\ =\frac{17}{9}+\frac{24}{9} \\ =\frac {17+24}{9} \\ =\frac{41}{9}}

Subtracting Fractions

Subtraction of fractions is similar to adding fractions. Find a common denominator, then subtract the numerators while keeping the denominator the same.

Example 3

Simplify.

\frac{3}{8} - \frac{13}{12}


Solution

For this problem, notice that the smallest number that is divisible by both 8 and 12 is 24. The common denominator is 24.

\displaylines{\frac{3}{8} - \frac{13}{12} = \frac{3}{8}\cdot\frac{3}{3} - \frac{13}{12}\cdot\frac{2}{2} \\ = \frac{9}{24}-\frac{26}{24} \\ = \frac {9 - 26}{24} \\ = \frac{-17}{24}}


Example 4

Simplify.

\frac{5}{7} - \frac{5}{3}
Solution

For this problem, notice that the smallest number that is divisible by both 7 and 3 is 21. The common denominator is 21.

\displaylines{\frac{5}{7} - \frac{5}{3} = \frac{5}{7}\cdot\frac{3}{3} - \frac{5}{3}\cdot\frac{7}{7} \\ = \frac{15}{21} - \frac{35}{21} \\ = \frac {15 - 35}{21} \\ = \frac{-20}{21}}

Multiplying Fractions

To multiply fractions, multiply the numerators and multiply the denominators.

Example 5

Multiply the fractions.

\frac{2}{5}\, \text{and} \frac{-4}{3}
Solution

Multiply the fractions by multiplying the numerators and multiplying the denominators:

\displaylines{\frac{2}{5}\cdot\frac{-4}{3} = \frac{2\cdot(-4)}{5\cdot3}\\ = \frac{-8}{15}}

Dividing Fractions

To divide fractions, invert and multiply. This means you take the second fraction, flip it upside down, then multiply the two resulting fractions.

Example 6

Divide:

\frac{3}{4}\div\frac{6}{5}
Solution

Perform the division by inverting the second fraction and then multiplying.

\displaylines{\frac{3}{4}\div\frac{6}{5} = \frac{3}{4}\cdot\frac{5}{6}\\ = \frac{3\cdot5}{4\cdot6}\\ = \frac{15}{24}\\ = \frac{5}{8} }

Order of Operations

Have you ever come across a question like this on social media?

If you view the comments on a post such as this, there is usually a great deal of disagreement on the answer. To arrive at the correct answer, you use the order of operations. The order of operations is a rule that helps you know the order in which to apply mathematical operations (addition, subtraction, multiplication, division) to simplify an expression. The order of operations uses the following four steps:

Step 1: Carry out operations enclosed in parentheses.

Step 2: Carry out operations involving exponents.

Step 3: Carry out any multiplication or division, in the order in which these operations appear from left to right.

Step 4: Carry out any addition or subtraction, in the order in which these operations appear from left to right.

PEMDAS

A helpful way to remember the order of operations is to use a mnemonic, or memory device as a tool. The acronym (an abbreviation formed from the initial letters of other words and pronounced as a word) PEMDAS, can be used to learn and remember the order of operations. PEMDAS stands for these words:

  • P : Parentheses

  • E : Exponents

  • MD : Multiplication and division (from left to right)

  • AS : Addition and subtraction (from left to right)

Even though the M appears before the D and the A appears before the S , don’t forget that multiplication and division are carried out in the order they appear from left to right. Addition and subtraction must also be carried out in the order they appear from left to right.

The acronym PEMDAS might be better written as PE(MD)(AS). Using the parentheses can help remind you that multiplication and division occur together, as do addition and subtraction.

Examples With Integers

Example 7

Apply the order of operations to the social media post from above.

7 - 1\times0 + 3\div3
Solution

Steps 1 and 2: As there are no parentheses or exponents, these steps do not apply.

Step 3: Start with Step 3 and perform all multiplication and division in the order in which they appear from left to right. Because

1\times0=0\, \text{and}\, 3\div3 = 1

you can rewrite the equation as

7 - 0 + 1

Step 4: Now carry out the addition and subtraction in the order in which they appear. This gives the answer:

7-0+1=7+1=8

Using the order of operations, you can determine that the answer is 8.

Example 8

Use the order of operations to simplify the following expression.

3(4-1)^2 -10\div2+3
Solution

Step 1 : Simplify the expression in the parentheses:

3(3)^2 -10\div2+3

Step 2 : Apply any exponents:

3\times9-10\div2+3

Step 3 : Apply multiplication and division in order from left to right:

27-5+3

Step 4 : Carry out the addition and subtraction in order from left to right:

25

Examples with Variables

Example 9

When using the order of operations in algebra, you often see variables in the expressions that you will need to simplify. Simplify the following expression:

3x-4(x-3)+2x
Solution

Step 1 : Simplify the expression in parentheses. In this case,

x-3

is already in its simplest form, so you can’t simplify it further.

Step 2 : This doesn’t apply because there are no exponents.

Step 3 : There is no division, so move on to multiplications. Multiply the 4 by the

x-3

and use the distributive property to get

3x-4x+12+2x

Step 4 : This step involves addition and subtraction, but you can only combine like terms. This gives the answer:

x+12

Example 10

Often, instead of using the ÷ symbol, expressions are written as fractions. If possible, simplify the following expression:

\frac{3x-4}{3}
Solution

When an expression is written as a fraction, it is implied (hinted but not directly expressed) that parentheses are found around both the numerator and denominator of the fraction. Recognize that you don’t need to add the parentheses on the problem; however, you should recognize that the unwritten parentheses indicate in what order to solve the problem.

The expression should look like this:

\frac{(3x-4)}{(3)}

or

(3x-4)\div(3)

You must subtract 4 from the

3x

before you can divide by 3 because Step 1 tells you to perform the operation in parentheses first, including implied parentheses.

Since

3x-4

cannot be simplified, the entire expression is already as simple as possible. The 3 in the numerator and the 3 in the denominator cannot “cancel” each other because the order of operations with the implied parentheses in the fraction bar indicates that you must subtract 4 before dividing.

Exponent Rules

Here are four important rules that describe how to work with exponents:

Rule

Example

1. Product Rule

x^ax^b= x^{a+b}

2. Power of a Power Rule

(x^a)^b=x^{ab}

3. Power of a Product Rule

(xy)^a=x^ay^a

4. Negative Exponent Rule

x^{-a}=\frac{1}{x^a}

NOTE : You can apply the exponent rules in any order to simplify your answer.

Examples of Exponent Rules

Example 11

Simplify the following expression:

\frac{(3xy)^3}{x^2}
Solution

First, use Rule 2 to simplify the numerator of the fraction:

\frac{3^3x^3y^3}{x^2}

You know

3^3=27

so you can simplify:

\frac{27x^3y^3}{x^2}

Next, use Rule 4 to rewrite the expression without a fraction:

27x^3y^3x^{-2}

Finally, use Rule 1 to combine the x terms:

27x^{3+(-2)} y^3

This gives you the final answer:

27xy^3

Example 12

Simplify the following expression:

\frac {2x^4 (yz)^3} {(xz)^3y^4}
Solution

By applying the exponent rules, you get the following answer:

\displaylines{\frac{2x^4 (yz)^3} {(xz)^3y^4} = \frac {2x^4 (y^3) z^3} {x^3z^3y^4} \\ =2x^4 y^3 z^3 x^{-3} z^{-3} y^{-4}\\=2x^{4+-3} y^{3+-4} z^{3+-3}\\ =2x^1y^{-1}z^0}\\
=\frac{2x}{y}

However, it can also be seen in a moment of inspiration, a feeling of reassurance, or a quiet prompting. Sometimes the Hand of the Lord in our lives may be difficult to recognize and sometimes it is clearer.
