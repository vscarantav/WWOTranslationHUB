Chapter 7 Invitation Reflection

Overview

  • Task: You will analyze your personal experience with spiritual invitations from WRIT 101. You will examine what you learned from engaging with these elements. You will create specific action plans for continued faith-learning integration. Your reflection must follow the exploration-reflection-action framework from this week’s chapter reading.
  • Purpose: To reflect on your learning, connect spiritual insights to your experience, and plan how to apply them moving forward.
  • Length: 500–600 words

Instructions

Step 1: Review

Skim through Weeks 1–6 course materials. Identify specific spiritual invitations you remember. Look for weekly spiritual thoughts, “Seek Learning by Study and by Faith” sections, “The Hand of the Lord” daily reflection opportunities, “Think Celestial” reminders, and scripture references throughout materials

Step 2: Draft Stage 1 Exploration (200 words)

Identify 3–4 specific spiritual invitations across any of the content you reviewed. Describe your initial responses and typical approach to these spiritual elements.

Step 3: Draft Stage 2 Reflection (250 words)

Analyze how engaging with spiritual invitations affected your approach to assignments. Evaluate which had the most influence. Identify patterns in your responses.

Step 4: Draft Stage 3 Action (150 words)

Specify one commitment about integrating faith and learning in future courses. Plan ongoing practice. Set measurable goals with specific actions and timelines.

Step 5: Connect and Polish

Add transition phrases between paragraphs. Begin each paragraph with a clear topic sentence. Include specific examples from assignments. Connect each point back to your overall growth as a learner.

Step 6: Requirements Checklist

Before you submit, verify:

  • Uses three-stage structure: Exploration (200 words), Reflection (250 words), Action (150 words)
  • References at least 3 specific weeks or spiritual invitations from the course
  • Includes concrete examples of your actual responses to spiritual elements
  • Written in paragraph form with smooth transitions (no bullet points)
  • Meets 500–600 word count requirement

Step 7: Submit the Assignment

  • Submit your assignment to W07 Invitation Reflection in Canvas, which is due by the end of the week.

Success Criteria

Excellent work will:

  • Follow the three-stage reflective writing structure with appropriate word distribution
  • Include specific, authentic examples from your semester experience
  • Demonstrate genuine self-analysis that goes beyond surface-level description
  • Create concrete, measurable action plans for continued growth
  • Use effective paragraph structure with smooth transitions between ideas

This assignment develops: Reflective writing skills and metacognitive awareness of faith-learning integration

Think Celestial

Your life is a time to learn. Each day you choose what your eternal life will be with your choices. President Russell M. Nelson (2023), a modern-day prophet, taught about these important principles. “Mortality is a master class in learning to choose the things of greatest eternal import. Far too many people live as though this life is all there is. However, your choices today will determine three things: where you will live throughout all eternity, the kind of body with which you will be resurrected, and those with whom you will live forever. So, think celestial.” As you live every day with your focus on eternity, you are choosing to live with our Father in Heaven again.

Tips for Being a Successful Online Student

Take Breaks: Taking regular breaks during study or after finishing assignments helps you avoid burnout. Breaks help you recharge your energy and maintain productivity. Taking a walk, spending time with family and friends, or engaging in a hobby will make you an effective student. Finding time to recharge between terms is also important. This allows you to return to your next classes with energy and focus.

Tips for Improving Your Writing

Embrace Revision: Successful final products often require multiple drafts and revisions. You should not be afraid to edit your work. Rearrange sentences, rewrite paragraphs, and delete content to improve clarity, coherence, and effectiveness.
