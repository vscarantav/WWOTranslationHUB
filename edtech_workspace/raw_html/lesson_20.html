Chapter 5 Draft: Writing Interview

Overview

  • Task : You will conduct a personal interview with someone you know who works in a professional setting, asking specific questions about their workplace writing experiences. Then you will write a four-section narrative that tells the story of your learning journey, comparing your initial assumptions with your interview discoveries and creating a concrete plan for developing your own professional writing skills.
  • Purpose: To explore workplace writing, reflect on what you learn from a professional, and connect those insights to your own career preparation.
  • Length : 750–950 words

This assignment asks you to write a narrative, not a report. The voice, structure, and reflection skills you are developing in Week 5 apply directly here. Your Interview Narrative section should read as a story of discovery, with a clear beginning, a developing middle, and a reflection at the end, just as your personal narrative assignment does.

Instructions

Step 1: Prepare

Write three brief paragraphs documenting your target profession, personal writing background, and initial assumptions about professional writing in your field. Include specific job titles and work environments, your current writing confidence level, and predictions about how much time professionals spend writing daily. This preparation section becomes the beginning of your narrative: the moment before you learned something new.

Step 2: Interview

Select someone you know personally who works in any professional role. Ask these four required questions exactly as written:

  1. How much time do you spend writing at work during a typical day or week?
  2. What specific types of writing do you complete regularly?
  3. How confident are you in your writing ability, and how did you develop these skills?
  4. How would stronger writing skills affect your work experience or career advancement?

Ask at least two additional follow-up questions based on their responses. Take detailed notes and ask for specific examples. Consider how cultural background, both yours and your interviewee’s, might influence perspectives on professional communication, workplace writing expectations, and communication styles.

Step 3: Write

Organize your assignment into four sections:

  • Context (150–200 words) : Describe your target profession, your writing background, your initial assumptions, and introduce your interviewee. This is the beginning of your narrative: set the scene and establish what you expected before the interview.
  • Interview Narrative (300–350 words) : Present the conversation with specific examples and quotes. Use the showing techniques from Week 5: let your interviewee’s actual words carry the story rather than summarizing what they said. Include at least two direct quotes.
  • Analysis (200–250 words) : Compare your assumptions with your discoveries. Identify the most surprising insight and explain why it surprised you. Every sentence in this section should connect to something specific your interviewee said, not to general observations about professional writing.
  • Growth Plan (100–150 words) : Identify specific skills to develop and concrete action steps. Be as specific here as Week 5 asks you to be in your narrative: not “I will improve my writing” but “I will practice writing professional emails by drafting one each week and asking my supervisor for feedback.”

Tips for Being a Successful Online Student

Set a Timer: It is easier to be highly productive for short periods, roughly 30 minutes. Using a timer to work without distractions for 30 minutes, followed by a 5-10-minute break or returning the next day, often allows you to accomplish more than working for a few hours without a break.

Tips for Improving Your Writing

Feedback is a Gift: Receiving feedback or edits on an assignment can be difficult and overwhelming. However, each comment is an opportunity to improve. Reading and editing a paper is a significant undertaking, and it is a gift when someone gives their time to help you improve.

A Note on AI and This Assignment

The analytical work in this assignment depends entirely on what your interviewee said and what you genuinely discovered. AI cannot conduct your interview, know your personal assumptions, or supply the specific surprise that makes your Analysis section worth reading.

Before applying any AI assistance, read your Analysis section and ask whether every sentence connects to something specific your interviewee said. A general statement like “professionals write more than students expect” could have been written without doing the interview. Replace any sentence like that with something that reflects what you heard.

A growth accelerator for this assignment sounds like this: “Here is my Analysis section. Are there sentences that make general statements rather than connecting specifically to what my interviewee said? Point them out without rewriting.”

A dependence trap sounds like this: “Write an analysis comparing these interview notes to my initial assumptions.” Complete all four sections yourself. After finishing, you may use AI to check grammar and spelling using these approved prompts: “Please identify grammar and spelling errors in this paragraph without changing my ideas: [paste your text]” “Which sentences in this paragraph could be clearer? Please point them out without rewriting: [paste your text]” “Help me express this idea more clearly in English while keeping my original meaning: [paste specific sentence]”
