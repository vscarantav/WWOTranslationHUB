Chapter 6 Writing Project: Personal Narrative Revision

Overview

  • Task : You will apply the three-stage revision process (big picture, paragraph-level, sentence-level) to substantially improve your personal narrative from Week 4. Then write a structured 250-word reflection analyzing your revision decisions, how you integrated WriteGrader feedback, and what you learned about your writing process.
  • Purpose: To improve your narrative through revision and reflect on how feedback and intentional changes strengthen your writing.
  • Length : 250-word reflection + revised narrative

Instructions

Step 1: Analyze

Review your Week 5 narrative and your WriteGrader feedback (your automated writing feedback from the Week 5 submission). Identify the three most important areas needing improvement and create a written revision plan. Consider how your cultural background might influence your approach to feedback.

Step 2: Revise

Apply systematic three-stage revision process:

Stage 1 — Big Picture (60–75 minutes): Focus on narrative structure and central message. Does your story build toward clear insight or change? Is your opening engaging and conclusion meaningful? Are all events needed for your main story thread? Reorganize for better narrative flow if needed.

Stage 2 — Paragraph-Level (45–60 minutes): Add specific sensory details that create vivid scenes. Provide context for people or places unfamiliar to readers. Strengthen transitions between sections. Confirm that each paragraph advances your narrative purpose.

Stage 3 — Sentence-Level (30–45 minutes): Vary sentence structures for engaging rhythm. Replace vague language with concrete details. Balance informal expression with academic appropriateness while maintaining your authentic voice throughout.

Step 3: Polish

Complete technical formatting (APA style, title page), final proofreading, and confirm proper file naming (w06_personal_narrative_revision-FirstName_LastName) and PDF conversion. Use your program’s APA Style Guide for formatting requirements.

Step 4: Reflect

Write a 250-word reflection in three structured paragraphs:

  • Paragraph 1 (80–100 words): Describe major changes using specific language like “I reorganized paragraphs 3 and 4 for better flow”
  • Paragraph 2 (80–100 words): Explain how you applied course concepts and which revision strategies proved most effective
  • Paragraph 3 (70–90 words): Share insights about your writing process that you will apply in future work

Your written revision plan from Step 1 is the foundation of your reflection in Step 4. If you completed Step 1 in writing before opening any AI tool, your reflection will be specific because you recorded your decisions before making them. If you skipped the written plan and revised directly, your reflection will likely stay at the surface level, describing changes in general terms rather than explaining the reasoning behind specific decisions. Complete the written revision plan first. Then revise. Then reflect on what you actually decided and why. This sequence is what the assignment assesses, and it is the sequence AI cannot replicate for you.

Step 5: Requirements Checklist

Before you submit, verify:

  • Applied a three-stage revision process to original narrative with substantial improvements
  • Addressed most important WriteGrader feedback points from Week 5
  • Maintained authentic voice while improving clarity and academic appropriateness
  • Included 250-word structured reflection using specific metacognitive language
  • Followed APA formatting, correct file naming, and PDF submission requirements

Step 6: Submit the Assignment

  • Submit your assignment to W06 Writing Project in Canvas, which is due by the end of the week.

English Writing: Mastering the Basics

Commas in the Greetings and Closings of Letters

Greetings are separated by commas. When you write an e-mail or a letter, you add a comma after the greeting word or the person’s name. Examples include “Dear Mr. Smith,” or “Hello,” or another greeting. You also need to include a comma after the closing. The closing is the word or phrase you put before your signature. Examples include “Sincerely,” or “With thanks,”.

Transitions

A strong paragraph moves seamlessly from the topic sentence into the supporting sentences and on to the concluding sentence. To help organize a paragraph and ensure that ideas logically connect, writers use transitional words and phrases. A transition is a connecting word that describes a relationship between ideas. Examples include “Equally important,” “Finally,” “In addition,” and other words that indicate a transition.

AI Usage Policy

This assignment develops metacognitive awareness of your personal writing process and authentic reflection on your individual revision experience. AI cannot know your genuine response to feedback, your specific learning journey, or your authentic voice and personal story. The learning objectives focus on building self-awareness as a writer and documenting your real revision decisions, which only come from your own thinking and reflection.

PROHIBITED : Having AI write content, ideas, or analysis for you

  • Why prohibited: AI cannot provide genuine insights about your personal writing process, authentic response to feedback, or real learning from the revision experience

ASK FIRST : Translation assistance or major editing questions

  • Why restricted: Changes should preserve your original voice, personal insights, and authentic revision experience

APPROVED : Grammar and spelling identification after completing your draft

  • Why allowed: Technical writing support does not compromise your authentic revision insights and helps all students communicate their ideas clearly in academic English
Approved AI Prompts

Use these prompts to stay in control of your work:

  • Grammar Check : “Please identify grammar and spelling errors in this paragraph without changing my ideas or voice: [paste your text]”
  • Clarity Review: “Which sentences in this paragraph could be clearer? Please point them out without rewriting my content: [paste your text]”
  • Translation Support: “Help me express this idea more clearly in English while keeping my original meaning and personal voice: [paste specific sentence]”
