Quick Overview 

What: Reflect on your writing journey this semester. Analyze one challenging assignment. Then, drawing from your broader term experience, identify a key insight about yourself as a writer and create a specific improvement plan for future work. 

Length: 400–500 words 

Task: You will write a personal reflection using the three-stage reflective writing process from Chapter 7: exploration, reflection, and action. Choose your most challenging assignment from this course. Analyze what you learned about yourself as a writer. Create a specific plan for continued growth. Base your reflection on your authentic experiences in WRIT 101. 

Step-By-Step Process 

Step 1: Gather Review your assignments from Weeks 1–6. Look at instructor feedback. Check Chapter 7 preparation materials. 

Step 2: Explore (approximately 150 words) Choose one of your WRIT 101 assignments to focus on: the Life Story Draft, One Story Two Audiences, Email for Help, Strategic Communication, Family History Research Interview, Personal History Project, Writing Interview, or Personal Narrative. Describe your approach, initial thoughts, and specific difficulties you encountered. Explain which assignment was most challenging for you. Describe what made it specifically difficult. Tell how you approached it. 

Step 3: Reflect (approximately 150 words) Go past describing what happened. Analyze what you learned. Focus on one meaningful discovery. Explain how this insight changed your thinking or approach to writing. Analyze what surprised you most during this course. Think about writing or yourself as a writer. What insight emerged that you did not expect? 

Step 4: Plan (approximately 150 words) Apply SMART goal framework with at least 3 concrete action steps. Specify resources you will use. Set a realistic timeline for implementation. Create a specific action plan for one writing skill you want to develop further in your next course or professional work. 

Example SMART goals: 

  • I will practice introducing and explaining every quote or paraphrase I use, rather than dropping it in without context. Action steps:  (1) use the writing center or an online guide to practice the quote-sandwich method, (3) before submitting, check that every source I cited is explained in my own words after the quote. Timeline: apply to all research assignments next semester. 
  • I will write clearer emails and reports for academic or professional settings. Action steps: (1) read one short article per week on professional writing, (2) ask a professor, advisor, or colleague to review one piece of writing per month, (3) keep a personal list of phrases that work well and ones that caused confusion. Timeline: first two months of next semester or new job. 

Step 5: Revise Review your reflection for paragraph structure, specific examples, and clarity. 

Before submitting, apply one final self-check to your reflection: read each sentence and ask whether it describes something that only you could have written about this course. Sentences like “I learned that writing requires careful planning” could appear in any student’s reflection in any writing course.  

Sentences like “I realized my thesis for the Personal History draft was too broad only after my instructor pointed out that my second paragraph was answering a different question than my first” describe something specific to your experience. Replace any sentence that could belong to anyone with a sentence that belongs only to you. This is the standard your instructor will apply when reading your reflection, and it is the most reliable test you can run before submitting. 

Requirements Checklist Before you submit, verify: 

  • Stage 1: Described one specific challenging assignment and what made it difficult for you personally 
  • Stage 2: Identified one meaningful insight about writing or yourself as a writer that surprised you 
  • Stage 3: Created a concrete action plan with specific steps and timeline for improvement 
  • Used paragraph form throughout (no bullet points in your essay) 
  • Included specific examples from your actual course assignments 

Success Criteria Excellent work will: 

  • Use specific examples from your actual course assignments rather than general statements 
  • Demonstrate authentic personal insight about your writing development 
  • Include a realistic, concrete action plan with specific steps and timeline 
  • Show clear progression through the three reflective writing stages 

AI Usage Policy 

This assignment develops reflective writing skills and metacognitive self-assessment. It requires authentic personal analysis. AI cannot know your real experiences in this course. AI cannot know your genuine challenges with specific assignments. AI cannot know your authentic insights about your growth as a writer. The learning objectives focus on self-awareness and reflective thinking skills. These skills only come from genuine analysis of your own learning journey. 

PROHIBITED: Having AI compose content, ideas, or analysis for you 

  • Why prohibited: AI cannot provide authentic insights about your personal learning experiences. AI cannot know genuine challenges you faced. AI cannot know real growth you have experienced in this course. 

ASK FIRST: Translation assistance or major editing questions 

  • Why restricted: Translation support should preserve your original meaning and personal voice in your reflection. 

APPROVED: Grammar and spelling identification after completing your draft 

  • Why allowed: Technical writing support does not compromise your authentic reflection. It helps all students communicate their personal insights clearly. 

Length and Structure Guidance 

Your reflection should be approximately 500 words in total. This length allows you to be thorough without becoming overwhelming. Think of it as telling a complete story without unnecessary details. 

Here is a helpful way to think about organizing your word count across the three sections: 

Section 1: What have you learned? (150 to 175 words) Write 3 to 5 sentences about each lesson. Each lesson needs a topic sentence. Add 1 to 2 supporting sentences with specific examples. You might find that some lessons need more explanation than others. That is perfectly fine. 

Section 2: What have you done well? (175 to 200 words) Write 2 to 3 sentences about each strength. Include a specific example from your assignments. Explain how this strength improved your writing. This section might feel a little longer because you are both describing and explaining. 

Section 3: What will you do to improve? (150 to 175 words) Write 3 to 4 sentences about each improvement area. State the weakness clearly. Describe your specific action plan with measurable steps. Remember, your plan does not need to be perfect. It simply needs to be honest and achievable. 

Approved AI Prompts Use these prompts to stay in control of your work: 

  • Grammar Check: “Please identify grammar and spelling errors in this paragraph without changing my ideas: [paste your text]” 
  • Clarity Review: “Which sentences in this paragraph could be clearer? Please point them out without rewriting: [paste your text]” 
  • Translation Support: “Help me express this idea more clearly in English while keeping my original meaning: [paste specific sentence]” 

Required Attribution If you use AI assistance, add this note to your submission: “[AI tool name] helped with [specific function] after I completed my draft. All ideas and content are my own work.” 

Why This Assignment Matters Reflective writing and self-assessment skills are useful for professional development — you will regularly need to evaluate your performance, identify growth areas, and articulate your development to supervisors.
