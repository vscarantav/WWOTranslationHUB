Student Outreach

Purpose

This course is designed to support and mentor students. The inclusion of certified graders in this course is to help you have more time to reach out and provide one-to-one student support. Refer back to the Student Outreach principles in your OL 110 WWB Instructor Certification course.

Overview

Student outreach involves any work building relationships with students, clarifying course content and assignments, and encouraging them to do their best. While specific instructions may vary, outreach generally includes:

  • Non-participating and Failing Students - In the initial weeks of a course, and at key times surrounding academic deadlines, reach out to students who are in danger of failing the course due to low participation or failing grades. Message templates are provided in relevant weeks.
  • Missing Assignments - Starting in Week 02, reach out to any students in your class who are missing high-stakes assessments. Specific assessments will be identified in your course-specific outreach tasks.
  • Low Performing Students - Beginning in Week 02, reach out to students who have scored low on high-stakes assessments, focusing on guiding them through specific concepts to help them succeed. You may also consider allowing them to resubmit their assignments.
  • Positive Outreach - While you are focusing on students who need additional help, don't forget to build relationships and a presence with those who are already succeeding by reaching out to students and recognizing their successes.
  • Inspired Outreach - As an instructor in your course, pray for your students. You may receive additional inspiration and guidance for other ways you may positively influence and encourage the students in your class.

Best Practice: Use students' personal email, in addition to the Canvas Inbox, for your outreach communication. If a student has a personal email available, you can locate it via the Contact List in your Canvas course menu.

Tracking

As you go about your weekly student outreach tasks, take care to not duplicate messages to students. Develop a method of tracking your outreach. Use tools like the "Notes" column in the Gradebook, Google Sheets, or Excel to record student communications. Choose a system that works best for you. Here is a template you can use.

Centrally Managed Graders

This course uses centrally managed, certified graders for all grading. The graders are managed by the Online Instruction Grading team. Please speak kindly of the graders to the students as they are a critical part of the Instructional Team. Communication paths regarding graders are outlined in the “Be a Team Player” section of your OL 110 WWB Instructor Certification.

Centralized Grading Guidelines

  • Grade Adjustments: Instructors should not adjust grades in the Gradebook. Support the grades and feedback awarded. If you disagree with a grade, contact the ACL or your supervisor.
  • Grading Order: Graders grade student submissions for all sections on a first in, first out basis. Assignments enter into the queue tool from multiple course sections in the order of their submission, and graders work to get students feedback as quickly as possible after submission, rather than after the due date. You may have multiple graders grading your students' work. Graders strive to complete all previous week’s grading by each Wednesday at 6:59 AM UTC.
  • Grade Posting: Grades are posted automatically. Do not adjust this Gradebook setting.
  • Resubmissions: Students can resubmit assignments 1-2 times based on assignment settings, allowing them to correct mistakes such as submitting the wrong file. Instructors can allow additional submissions, but this must be done for the entire class. Although this change affects all students, typically only those needing extra submissions will notice, and it usually does not create issues.
  • Academic Dishonesty: Graders will note any potential cheating or plagiarism in the Plagiarism and Outreach Queue. The CGL will notify the instructor. It is the instructor's responsibility to determine if plagiarism has occurred, address it with the student, and decide how it will be handled in accordance with your program’s Academic Dishonesty Approach.

Disruptive Students

Occasionally, instructors encounter students who are disruptive to the classroom teaching environment. Instructors can promote a respectful, safe, and engaging online classroom by setting clear expectations, maintaining a strong presence, and addressing disruptive behavior calmly and kindly. Many student issues stem from misunderstanding or inexperience and can often be resolved through private, patient correction and open communication. When serious issues arise, instructors should follow institutional policies, collaborate with their AIM, and document the situation, focusing on creating a positive learning environment for all students.

If instructors cannot resolve the situation themselves, they can direct students to submit a complaint or grievance to BYU-Pathway: Student Grievances. Instructors can submit concerns with student behavior to Pathway Student Wellness

Course Notes

Dates and Late Work

Worldwide Online classes adhere to the following policies created to help international students succeed. These policies are not listed in the syllabus, but you can let individual students know about these policies as needed.

  1. All assignments are open from the beginning of the course.
  2. All assignments (except perhaps group assignments) have until dates with this pattern:
    • Weeks 01-04: the until date is 14 days after the due date
    • Weeks 05-07: the until date is Thursday of Week 7
  3. See your syllabus for any potential Late Penalties in your course.
  4. To motivate students to turn assignments in on time, assignments not submitted by the due date receive an automatic zero.

If a student submits an assignment late, they may notice they received a zero on the assignment and think there is nothing they can do to change the grade and there is no point in turning in the assignment late. Instructors can help struggling students succeed by encouraging them to turn in late work by the Until Date for that assignment.

Every Week

Facilitate the course and coach students to find success. You won't be grading, so your time should be spent researching the gradebook, making note of what feedback will help students on assignments, encouraging the class through weekly announcements, and messaging individuals or groups of students. The majority of your time will be spent on student outreach.

Here's a general schedule of what to expect each week:

  • Daily: check email and respond to announcement questions/discussions and comments on assignments.
  • Before the week begins:
    • Review that week's Teaching Notes thoroughly.
    • Post an announcement for the current week.
    • New instructors are encouraged to work through the assignments before the students, so you are able to answer questions.
  • During the week:
    • Complete student outreach.
    • Participate in class discussions.
    • Watch for ungraded assignments. If you have assignments in your course waiting longer than seven days to be graded, please contact the ACL (or your supervisor if you are an international instructor) and do not grade them yourself.
